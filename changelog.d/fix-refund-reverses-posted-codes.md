### Fix: a refund reverses a posted invoice on the tax codes it was posted with (sales)
- Once a sales invoice is posted, its refunds and credit notes now reverse its goods lines, its discount (the per-rate parts of an apportioned order discount, or the single header-rate discount line) and its shipping on the accounting tax codes the invoice was posted with, read from the order's newest ledger-held invoice record, not on the rate's current mapping. A tax rate re-mapped to another code after posting (an in-place edit, so no rate keeps the old code) only affects invoices not yet posted, so a credit note can no longer move VAT between codes or VAT boxes. The posted code is priced at the percentage of the rate the record binds it to. The posted-VAT fence, the accounting retry fence, the manual (quarantined WooCommerce) refund recording, the credit-note builder and the credit-note display read the same posted codes.
- When there is no usable record, the rate's current code is read as before: no posted invoice, an invoice posted by Xeroom or linked by hand, a newest record that is in flight (an update accepted but not yet confirmed falls back to the current code even though the ledger may still hold the older version), operator-asserted, not proven absent or unreadable (an older version is never read in its place; a shadow or never-sent failed update is skipped), an EU scheme or B2B zero-rate invoice, and goods lines with no tax rate. The record is the sync log, so it lasts only while the log is retained.
- This changes a live path: refunds of a posted invoice (goods, discount and shipping), whether or not the apportion switch is on. It is identical whenever the mapping has not moved since posting. The invoice update of an edited posted order still builds from current codes (it re-posts the document).
