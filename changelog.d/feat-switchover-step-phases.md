### Switchover: per-step phases, no-writer check, owner confirmations (cutover)
- Every row of the writer ownership map now names the switchover step that moves it to IMS: step M (Mintsoft, with the WooCommerce writeback the bridge also did: order status, tracking, partial shipments, product meta, withdrawal outcome, TrackShip, merge hold, ASN and order comments), step W (stock, webhooks, customer e-mail) or step X (Xero and the invoice note and document). The tax rate stays with the operator.
- The producer decision reads the live-from instant of the row's step, so the phase is P1, then P2-M, P2-W, P2-X. Nothing changes while no live-from instant is set.
- `npm run writers:check` derives the per-step phases and fails on a step set out of order, a step without the grant for a destination it writes, and an operation IMS owns in a live phase whose switch is off (new exit code 6, `step-gap`). It requires the evidence that each incumbent has stopped from step M on; a source that does not exist yet fails with "evidence source not built" and a probe that did not answer fails as "not-probed". Bridge contests are derived from the map.
- New `WriterAttestation` table, `npm run writers:attest` and an administrator screen (the Owner confirmations page at `/sync/writer-confirmations`) for the owner's confirmations that the Qoblex stock push and Qoblex's Xero posting are stopped, with an activity-log entry. Turning on `wc_stock_sync_enabled` is refused without the stock confirmation. `xero_sync_enabled` is not guarded (P1 reads from Xero with it on); instead the delivery of step-X writes (Xero and the invoice note and document) and of the step-W stock push is held until `qoblex-xero-stopped` / `qoblex-stock-push-stopped` is recorded, and `writers:check` fails P2-W and P2-X without them, even if `wc_stock_sync_enabled` is already on. A confirmation that is absent, or not read yet after a start or a failed read, HOLDS the queued work and retries it; it never turns it into a shadow. The snapshot is process-wide (`globalThis`), so routes, actions, crons and scripts see one refresh. The confirmation row and its audit-log entry are written in one transaction.
- The drain, the claim boundary and the writer floor are per operation, using each row's own step: with only step M live, the WooCommerce completions and tracking go out while stock, the invoice note and the invoice document stay held. The destination-level gate is only the earliest-open transport barrier.
- `writer_attestations` is `CONFIG_ALLOWED` for first-load, so a confirmation recorded before the initial load does not block it.
- **Upgrade note.** An installation that already sets `WC_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` or `MINTSOFT_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` changes behaviour: `WC_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` now governs only stock and webhooks (step W); order completions, tracking, partial shipments, product metadata and the WMS-status meta read `MINTSOFT_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` (step M), and the invoice note and document read `XERO_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` (step X). `WC_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` without `MINTSOFT_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` fails `writers:check`; `MINTSOFT_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` with the WooCommerce grant and no `WC_WRITES_LIVE_FROM` (formerly held) now writes to WooCommerce, and `writers:check` warns. Both are also logged at startup. Installations in P1 (nothing set) are unchanged; see docs/installation.md, "Upgrading an installation that already sets WC_WRITES_LIVE_FROM or MINTSOFT_WRITES_LIVE_FROM".
- Work held for an owner confirmation (the step-X Xero rows and the invoice note, the step-W stock push) is now handed back without spending an attempt. The Xero drains (outbox and direct) ask whether the row's delivery is held BEFORE claiming anything: neither the outbox job nor the sync-log row is claimed, the job is offered again after a short fixed delay (90 seconds) with its attempt count untouched, and the direct selection leaves held types out so they cannot fill a batch. A row the hold refuses after the claim (the confirmation went away in between) releases its claim the same way. A stock or invoice-document push refused after the claim keeps its attempt count too. A queued job can therefore no longer reach PERMANENT_FAILED because the confirmation took longer than the retry budget to arrive.
