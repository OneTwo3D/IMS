### CRM foundation: customer identity, company grouping, WooCommerce customer id per order, customer timeline
- **One person across records.** Customer records whose emails match after folding case, spaces, `+tag` and Gmail/Googlemail dots are shown as one person on the customer page. Nothing is merged or deleted: the originals stay, joins are computed on read, and every automatic join is audited in the new insert-only `customer_merge_events` table. An anonymised record or a placeholder email never joins. WooCommerce cannot hold one email on two accounts, so aliases (`+tag`, Gmail dots) join even when the two records carry different WooCommerce customer ids. MANAGER and ADMIN can merge two records or separate one (audited, needs a recent sign-in and a confirmation that shows both customers' names and emails); the latest decision for a pair wins.
- **Company grouping.** The customer page lists other customers with the same billing company name or company email domain (free-mail domains never count). Legal suffixes fold away when comparing (Ltd/Limited, Inc/Incorporated, plc, LLC, GmbH, Co/Company). A view only.
- **Customer timeline.** Orders, cancellations, invoices, payments, credit notes and refunds, refund payments, expected and received returns for the person or the single record, read-only, newest first, paged. Visible to every sales user.
- **WooCommerce customer id per order.** New nullable `sales_orders.external_customer_id`, filled by the order importer on create and on later deliveries (a positive id only, never on an erased order). Cleared by an erasure. No backfill in the migration; `npm run crm:backfill-order-customer-id` (dry run unless `--apply`) fills it from deliveries already stored, in batches, with one audit entry and a count of the orders no stored delivery can fill; no WooCommerce call, `updatedAt` not raised.
- **Importer customer match.** An order is matched to its customer first by the WooCommerce customer id (the link, or the id stored on that customer's earlier orders), then by email: an exact comparison ignoring case and surrounding spaces (`lower(btrim(email))`, index `customers_email_lower_btrim_idx`), never a pattern match, so `j_smith@` and `j.smith@` are different customers.
- **Erasure covers the whole person.** Anonymising a customer erases every live record in its cluster, each by the existing per-customer erasure with its own record. The members are saved on a request row before the first is erased, so a partial failure is reported and running the erasure again finishes every member (also a member reachable only through one already erased). Members that link others go last, the requested customer at the very end. While such a request is unfinished, merge and unmerge refuse its members. The confirmation lists every record by name and email.
- `npm run crm:identity-report` now also reports how many clusters the rule forms and lists a sample of them as customer ids only. Read-only; the owner has approved running it on production.
- Migrations (additive): `20261119131000_crm_customer_identity` (column, two enums, audit table with insert-only triggers), `20261119131100_sales_orders_customer_created_idx` and `20261119131200_customers_identity_key_idx` (both `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY`), `20261119140000_customer_cluster_erasures` (the saved member list of an erasure), `20261119140100_crm_loose_identity_key_fn` (SQL twin of the email fold), `20261119140200`/`140300`/`140400` (three more `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY`: the fold, the importer email match, the storefront id on orders) and `20261119140500_crm_indexes_validate` (fails the deploy if any CRM concurrent index is missing or INVALID). `customers_identity_key_idx` is superseded and unused; drop it in a later cleanup migration.
