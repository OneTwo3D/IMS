
### Accounting

- **Reverse-charge bills: a net-payable figure.** The stored VAT and totals are unchanged (the VAT return needs them), but what is owed to the supplier is now the stored total less the VAT the bill accounts for itself on its reverse-charge lines. Mark as paid offers it by default, a bill's paid or part-paid status is judged against it, the unrealised exchange-rate revaluation uses it for the open supplier balance, the freight cancellation credit-note gate offsets credit notes against it, and the draft credit note raised for returned goods is grossed up to and limited to it. A bill without a reverse-charge line is unchanged.
- **Rounding line to the order total, behind `xero_round_to_order_total_enabled` (off by default).** With it on, a sales invoice raised from an order carries the order total and its single Rounding adjustment line is sized so the document totals exactly the order total, within the order reconciliation tolerance (never more than 1.00; beyond it the document is refused and nothing is sent). With it off nothing changes. The posting key of a document is the same with the setting on or off.
- **Reverse-charge bills: reports and the pay dialog use the net payable.** AP aging and the FX gain/loss report count a reverse-charge bill at its net payable, and the Mark Bill as Paid dialog and the bill row show it (with the bill total as secondary text).
- **Rounding to the order total also covers credit notes.** A full-refund or chargeback credit note carries the same Rounding line as the invoice it reverses, so it totals what the invoice does. This follows how the invoice was posted (read from its own sync row), not the setting's value when the credit note is raised, so turning the setting on or off between the invoice and the credit note never makes them disagree.
